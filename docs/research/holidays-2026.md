## 1. Switzerland (26 cantons)

**Legend**
- **✓** = canton-wide public holiday and *den Sonntagen gleichgestellt* (Art. 20a ArG; SECO list, Stand 12.12.2024)
- **✓°** = canton-wide official public holiday (*öffentlicher Ruhetag* / *jour férié officiel*) under cantonal law, but **not** gleichgestellt under the Labour Act. Shops and noise rules follow the Sunday regime, but employers may require work. **You need to decide whether to show these.**
- **✓*** = canton-wide, but only under a condition (see notes)
- **½** = afternoon only
- **partly** = only in some districts or municipalities (treat as not canton-wide)
- **–** = not a cantonal holiday

**Notes (CH)**
- **The 8-day cap:** federal law (Art. 20a ArG) lets a canton make at most **8 days a year** Sunday-equivalent, plus 1.8. Several cantons have more rest days than that, and those extras are the ✓° entries.
- **Confession- and district-based cantons**
  - **AG:** there is no canton-wide list. Holidays are set **per district** (Bezirk) or municipality. Canton-wide are only 1.1, Karfreitag, Auffahrt, 1.8 and 25.12.
  - **FR:** canton-wide are only 1.1, Karfreitag, Auffahrt, 1.8 and 25.12.
    - Catholic-majority communes add Fronleichnam, 15.8, 1.11 and 8.12.
    - Reformed-majority communes add 2.1, Ostermontag, Pfingstmontag and 26.12.
  - **SO:** Fronleichnam, 15.8 and 1.11 do not apply in **Bezirk Bucheggberg**. 1.5 counts from 12:00 only.
- **Conditional days (✓*)**
  - **NE:** 2.1 and 26.12 count **only if they fall on a Monday**, so in 2026 neither applies. Fronleichnam applies only in Le Landeron.
  - **AR:** 26.12 is dropped when 25.12 falls on a Monday or Friday. In 2026, 25.12 is a Friday, so **26.12 is not a holiday**.
  - **AI:** 26.12 is dropped "if it would create three consecutive rest days". In 2026 this is ambiguous (Friday 25.12, Saturday 26.12, Sunday 27.12).
- **Local-only days**
  - **AI:** Mauritiustag (22.9) applies only in the inner part of the canton (*innerer Landesteil*).
  - **LU:** 19.3 applies only where a municipality declares it.
  - **GR:** 29.6 is not in cantonal law and can only be a local Ruhetag.
  - **VS:** Mauritiustag was not found in cantonal law.
- **Not in any cantonal list**
  - **Bettagsmontag:** VD only.
  - **Berchtoldstag:** absent in ZH, SH, LU, ZG, SZ, UR, SO, GR and others. **TG** made 2.1 and 26.12 statutory rest days in its new **Ruhetagsgesetz, in force since 1.1.2026**, which also adds 1.5 as a rest day (✓°).
- **Sundays left out:** Ostersonntag, Pfingstsonntag and Bettag (3rd Sunday in September) are "high holidays" in most cantons. They are omitted because they fall on Sundays anyway.

## 2. Austria (AT-1 … AT-9)

| movable | Fronleichnam |
| 15.8. | Mariä Himmelfahrt |
| 26.10. | Nationalfeiertag |
| 1.11. | Allerheiligen |
| 8.12. | Mariä Empfängnis |
| 25.12. | Christtag |

- **Karfreitag is no longer a holiday for anyone.** § 7 Abs. 3 ARG, the old Karfreitag rule for Protestant, Old Catholic and Methodist employees, was repealed by **BGBl. I Nr. 22/2019**. It was replaced by the **"persönlicher Feiertag"** (§ 7a ARG): one self-chosen day a year, taken from the employee's annual leave.
- **24.12 and 31.12 are not holidays** (AK).
- **Allerseelen (2.11)** is a school holiday, not a public holiday.

**State patron-saint days (Landespatrone)** — these are **not statutory public holidays.** They are not in the ARG, and oesterreich.gv.at and katholisch.at state this explicitly. They are typically school-free and many state offices close.

**Notes (DE)**
- **Ostersonntag / Pfingstsonntag**
  - **BB** names both explicitly (§ 2 FTG BB).
  - **HE** only covers them because § 1 HFeiertagsG declares *all Sundays* gesetzliche Feiertage. No other state names them. They are irrelevant for a calendar, since they fall on Sundays anyway.
- **BY, Mariä Himmelfahrt:** a holiday only in municipalities with a Catholic majority (Art. 1 Abs. 1 Nr. 2, Abs. 3 FTG). The Bavarian statistics office (LfStat) publishes the list, based on Census 2022 and valid from 15.8.2025: **1,708 of 2,056 municipalities have it, 348 do not.** The holiday covers about 83% of municipalities, but by your rule it is "partly", so mark it not state-wide. If you want municipality precision later, use the LfStat list.
- **SN, Fronleichnam:** applies only in **25 former municipalities** (now in Landkreis Bautzen, the Sorbian Catholic area), listed in the FronleichnamsVO of 1993/1995.
- **TH, Fronleichnam:** applies only where it was observed in 1994, by the transitional rule § 10 ThürFGtG (in practice Eichsfeld). There is no official municipality list.
- **Changes since 2023**
  - **MV:** Frauentag added by the law of 7.7.2022, first observed **8.3.2023**.
  - **BE:** **one-off holidays** were added by the law of 10.7.2024: **8.5.2025** (already past) and **17.6.2028** (75 years since the 1953 uprising). Your engine needs to support single-year holidays.
  - **BY:** a bill to make 15.8 a holiday across Bavaria was **rejected** on 11.03.2026.
  - **Otherwise** there are no new permanent holidays in any state for 2023–2026.
